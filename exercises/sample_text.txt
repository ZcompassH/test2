THE CITY AT SIX IN THE MORNING

The city wakes slowly.  A bus, then another bus, then the sound of
shutters rolling up. By seven, the street is full of people who all
seem to know exactly where they are going.

***

Every morning is a small experiment in coordination. 3,000 commuters
cross the same bridge between 7:30 and 9:00. The bridge was built for
2,000. Nobody planned this; it simply happened, one apartment block at
a time.

What makes a neighbourhood work? Planners talk about density,
diversity, and design. Residents talk about the bakery, the school,
and whether the crossing light gives them enough time. Both are right,
and it doesn't matter as much as we think.

A well-planned street is not the same as a lived-in street. The city
is not a machine. It is a conversation, and the conversation is always
slightly out of order.

The city wakes slowly. The city goes to sleep even more slowly.
